# SR 26-2: what it actually says

Primary sources (Federal Reserve):
- Cover letter: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm
- Guidance attachment: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602a1.pdf

Everything under "Verified" is quoted from those two documents. Anything I couldn't verify is labeled.

## Verified

| Fact | Source quote |
|---|---|
| Issued April 17, 2026, jointly by the Federal Reserve, OCC and FDIC | Cover letter |
| Replaces SR 11-7 (2011) and SR 21-8 (2021) | Cover letter |
| Most relevant to banks over $30B in assets | "This guidance is expected to be most relevant to banking organizations with over $30 billion in total assets." (attachment, p. 2–3) |
| **GenAI and agentic AI are out of scope** | "Generative AI and agentic AI models are novel and rapidly evolving. As such, they are not within the scope of this guidance." (attachment, p. 3) |
| What counts as a "model" | "a complex quantitative method, system, or approach that applies statistical, economic, or financial theories to process input data into quantitative estimates." (p. 3) |
| Not a model | "simple arithmetic calculations… as well as deterministic rule-based processes and software…" (p. 3) |
| Risk-based approach | "Assessing risk based on model materiality is an important consideration for sound model risk management." (p. 4) |
| Validation | "Model validation evaluates whether models perform as expected and includes an assessment of a model's reliability and its limitations." (p. 7) |
| Governance | "All components of the model lifecycle benefit from model governance that is supported by clear policies and effective controls." (p. 10) |
| Inventory | "An effective model inventory includes sufficient information to understand model risks…" (p. 11) |
| Vendor models | "An important element of model risk management is the validation of vendor products, either by internal or outside parties." (p. 11) |

## Why this is the trigger for the bank

My assumption going in was that SR 26-2 would add AI rules. **It does the opposite.** The new model-risk rulebook for large US banks explicitly leaves GenAI and agentic AI out.

So a large bank running agents now has:
- a refreshed model-risk program (inventory, validation, governance) for traditional models, and
- **no supervisory framework for its agents.** The auditors still have to answer "who approved this agent action, and what record does it leave?" with no rulebook to point to.

That gap is exactly what a CAE would forward internally, and it's what Reign's governed-AI story answers. The brief shouldn't say "new rules for your AI." It should say: "The new guidance carves agents out. Here's the gap that leaves in your audit coverage, and three questions your team should be asking."

## What I would not claim in a brief

- That SR 26-2 "regulates AI." It explicitly doesn't regulate GenAI or agentic AI.
- Any effective date or deadline. The cover letter doesn't state one that I found.
- Anything about the OCC's companion bulletin beyond it existing. I didn't read it.

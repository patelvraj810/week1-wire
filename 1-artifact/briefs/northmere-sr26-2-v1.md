# SR 26-2: what it means for Northmere Financial Group (fictional)

**For:** Priya Raman (fictional), Chief Audit Executive
**Trigger:** SR 26-2, Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management

## What changed
On April 17, 2026, the Federal Reserve, OCC and FDIC replaced SR 11-7 with SR 26-2, their revised guidance on model risk management.[1] It is aimed mainly at banking organizations with over $30 billion in total assets.[2] The guidance explicitly leaves generative and agentic AI out of scope: "Generative AI and agentic AI models are novel and rapidly evolving. As such, they are not within the scope of this guidance."[2]

## What it means for Northmere
- Northmere's US banking operations fall in the size range the guidance targets, so the model-risk program there will likely be reviewed against the refreshed standard for inventory, validation and governance.[2]
- Northmere has agents in pilot on internal operations (fictional). Under SR 26-2 those agents sit outside the model-risk framework, which means there is no supervisory framework for them and audit has nothing to test them against.
- It is worth noting that this creates a gap: the agent pilots can move faster than the controls around them, and the first time audit sees an agent action may be after it has already touched a customer or a ledger.

## Three questions your team could ask this quarter
1. Which of our AI agents are in production or pilot today, and are they in any inventory at all, given SR 26-2 excludes them from the model inventory?
2. For each agent action that touches a customer or a record, can we show who approved that class of action, what the agent did, and why?
3. Which internal policy, if not model-risk management, is the control framework for agentic AI, and who owns it?
